In 2023, Tanzer et al. introduced Machine Translation from One Book (MTOB), a benchmark for LLM translation based on a single grammar book for Kalamang, an extremely low-resource language. Kalamang was chosen for several reasons:
- It's a low-resource language with fewer than 200 speakers
- A grammar, small dictionary, and small parallel corpus exist thanks to linguist Eline Visser, who conducted field studies with natives over four years
- The only material on the Internet in Kalamang is Visser's book and several articles
- Native speakers approved researchers using their language for studies

The Kalamang paper compared several LLMs with a human translator. LLMs were tested with various inputs: Words only, Sentences only, Grammar only (in three variants of different length - full grammar book, medium, and short excerpts), and various combinations of these. The human (the paper's first author), who studied the same materials, outperformed all LLMs, with Claude 2 taking second place in both BLEU and chrF scores. Later, Google used MTOB in their Gemini report, where Gemini 1.5 Pro, their best model with the longest context window of 2 million tokens, showed better results than the human from the original paper.

## DIY Reproduction

I was interested in a cheap DIY reproduction of these results with the best models publicly available in December 2024. Could an ordinary user with access to only chat-based versions of flagship LLMs and zero knowledge of Kalamang reproduce them? I used ChatGPT (4o, o1-preview, and o1), Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini (1.5 Pro (2 versions), 2.0 flash experimental), Llama 3.2, and DeepSeek V3 to find out. Since I don't know Kalamang and can't assess English-to-Kalamang translation results, I chose to test only Kalamang-to-English. I used chat-based versions because they're publicly available, inexpensive, and my experience with these chatbots helped me judge the results.

I assessed all results myself using key metrics from the original paper. For each LLM, I used the maximum amount of text that fit into the context window, whether a short grammar or full grammar + dictionary + sentences. I used 25 sentences from the test set; the results are available in an encrypted archive (password: kalamang2024). To reproduce the results, see the Google Colab notebook in the [repo](https://github.com/zzappa/diy_mtob/tree/main).

## Prompting

I used the prompt from the original paper for most models:

```
Kalamang is a language spoken on the Karas Islands in West Papua. Translate
the following sentence from Kalamang to English: <kalamang sentence>. To 
help with translation, here is the Kalamang grammar book and a small 
dictionary.
```

Claude required an amended prompt as per the original paper:

```
Kalamang is a language spoken on the Karas Islands in West Papua. Translate 
the following sentence from Kalamang to English: <kalamang sentence>. To 
help with translation, here is the Kalamang grammar book and a small 
dictionary. 
Now write the translation. If you are not sure what the translation should be,
then give your best guess. Do not say that you do not speak Kalamang. If your
translation is wrong, that is fine, but provide a translation.
```

For Gemini, I tried both standard prompt and longer prompt from the Gemini report (pp. 144-145).
```
You are tasked with translating the following sentence from {source language}
to {target language}: "{input sentence}". You will be given a field linguistics
grammar book, a bilingual word list, and a collection of parallel 
{source language}/{target language} sentences to aid you. Here is the book,
"A grammar of Kalamang": 
START OF GRAMMAR BOOK {grammar book} END OF GRAMMAR BOOK
The grammar book is now over. Remember that you are tasked with translating 
the following sentence from {source language} to {target language}: "{input sentence}". 
Now here is the bilingual word list: 
START OF WORD LIST {word list} ... END OF WORD LIST
The bilingual word list is now over. Remember that you are tasked with translating
the following sentence from {source language} to {target language}: "{input sentence}".
You will use the same style as the parallel sentences immediately below. Now 
here is the collection of parallel sentences: 
START OF PARALLEL SENTENCES 
{source language}: {source sentence} {target language}: {target sentence} 
END OF PARALLEL SENTENCES 
The collection of parallel sentences is now over. Now translate the following sentence
from {source language} to {target language}, using the style from the parallel sentences
immediately above. Translate: "{input language}".
I understand that you may not be familiar enough with Kalamang to make a confident translation,
but please give your best guess. Respond with only the translation and no other text.
```

## Model performance and observations

All tested models except ChatGPT (o1-preview and o1) and Gemini (1.5 Pro and 2.0 Flash) provided detailed explanations for their translation attempts. For comparison purposes, I trimmed everything but the answers. I always used the first model response, and never asked additional questions or regenerated the answer.

- ChatGPT 4o worked with full attached grammar and dictionary but couldn't handle these texts added as plain text to the first message. It was fastest to answer but usually returned gibberish.
- ChatGPT o1-preview doesn't support attachments, so the full text of the short book was added in the first sentence. Think time ranged from 10 to 60 seconds.
- Claude 3.5 Sonnet handled the medium-length grammar attachment. It answered quickly, almost as fast as ChatGPT 4o, with better results. The main limitation was its low message limit for long context.
- Gemini 1.5 Pro has the longest context currently available to the public. The complete grammar book, dictionary, and parallel sentences used about 320k tokens out of 2 million. Response times ranged from 20 to 150 seconds; answers were mostly good, though quality decreased with longer thinking times. Interestingly, Gemini flagged several answers as "Hate" content.
- Gemini 2.0 Flash experimental performed worse than 1.5 Pro and averaged 60 seconds per question.
- Llama 3.2 90B (accessed via Poe) performed better than ChatGPT 4o but worse than other models.

All models hallucinated translations when tested without Kalamang materials added to the context.

Beyond standard metrics, I assessed translation quality from a user perspective. Claude, Gemini, and ChatGPT o1 all performed reasonably well. Claude's answers were more cautious, including ambiguities (his/hers, palm/hand) or multiple translation versions (I used the first version for metrics calculations).

Comparing the highest-context setting for each LLM:

* Claude 3.5 showed the best results, even with less context data than Gemini
* Gemini 1.5 Pro pro-002 and pro-001 are close, but worked with full materials and required more time for thinking
* ChatGPT o1 and o1-preview performed slightly worse with less data
* DeepSeek V3 performed surprisingly good, with results close to o1-preview and Gemini 2.0
* Llama 3.2 90B performed notably worse but better than ChatGPT 4o
* ChatGPT 4o produced mostly gibberish both with attached files and pasted grammar

The metrics align with qualitative assessment. Claude 3.5 achieved a BLEU score of 23.2 and chrF score of 51, outperforming other models despite using less context. Gemini 1.5 Pro followed closely with scores of 23.1 and 50.0 respectively. The most interesting findings are: ChatGPT 4o, despite being a capable model for many tasks, performed significantly worse than all other models, including Llama 3.2 90B; and Claude 3.5 Sonnet shows the best performance, even though it has smaller context window length and is not a "reasoning" model, and is much faster than both o1 and Gemini. I tested DeepSeek V3 later, and it showed surprisingly good results, outperforming Gemini 2.0 Flash in chrF.

## Translation examples

To illustrate the models' performance, I looked closer at three examples from the test set, demonstrating some interesting patterns in translations.

### Example 1
```
Original: Keir Sakina mu imunat na.
Ground truth: A lorikeet ate Sakina's family's banana.
```

This seemingly simple sentence showed significant variations in translation quality. While Claude 3.5 came closest with "The parrots ate Sakina's family's bananas." other models struggled with the basic structure. Some models, like ChatGPT 4o, produced completely unrelated content or even responses in the wrong language. Notably, models varied in their handling of plurals (banana/bananas) and tense (ate/eat/are eating). Only Claude and DeepSeek could handle the compound possessiveness ("Sakina's family's").

| Model | Context | Translation | Notes |
|-------|----------|-------------|--------|
| Original | - | Keir Sakina mu imunat na. | Kalamang text |
| Ground Truth | - | A lorikeet ate Sakina's family's banana. | Reference translation |
| Claude 3.5 | Gs + W + S | The parrots ate Sakina's family's bananas. | Close to ground truth, plural form |
| Claude 3.5 | Gm | Keir and Sakina ate bananas | Misinterpreted proper noun |
| ChatGPT 4o | Gs | Keir Sakina telah menyelesaikan pekerjaannya. | Wrong language/nonsensical |
| ChatGPT 4o | G + W | You speak to Sakina now. | Incorrect interpretation |
| ChatGPT o1-preview | Gs | Sakina's parrots eat their bananas. | Present tense, possessive changed |
| ChatGPT o1 | Gm + W + S | Sakina and her family ate the bananas. | Different subject structure |
| Gemini 1.5 Pro | G + W | Sakina's parrot ate the banana. | Singular form, close structure |
| Gemini 1.5 Pro (25 Nov) | G + W + S | Sakina's parrots eat bananas. | Present tense, generic |
| Gemini 1.5 Pro (9 Dec) | G + W + S | Sakina's parrots are eating the bananas. | Present continuous |
| Gemini 2.0 Flash exp | G + W + S | The birds Sakina ate were poisonous. | Incorrect interpretation |
| Llama 3.2 90B | Gs + S | Sakina's family's mangoes are ripe. | Different meaning |
| DeepSeek 3V | Gm + S | The parrot ate Sakina's family's food. | Close, used generic "food" but got everything else right. |

### Example 2
```
Original: Kaliset me Aina emun kewe anggasun me per nam.
Ground truth: If it rains the water in front of Aina's mother's house puddles.
```

This more complex sentence shows the models' ability to handle conditional statements and spatial relationships. ChatGPT o1 produced the closest translation, maintaining both the conditional aspect and the spatial relationship. Most models captured the core elements (mostly nouns: rain, Aina, house) but varied in how they represented the water's behavior (puddle, leak, collection). This example shows that models can produce notably different interpretations of spatial and temporal relationships.

### Example 3
```
Original: Tanggor me mal reidak.
Ground truth: There are many rabbitfish at Tanggor.
```

This example shows a consistent error across multiple models: the confusion between "rabbitfish" and "loincloths". This misinterpretation appeared in both Claude and Gemini outputs, suggesting a potential issue in the word list or dictionary interpretation. ChatGPT o1-preview produced the most accurate translation even with less context. This example shows how word-level ambiguity can affect translation quality regardless of context window size or model capability.

## Cost analysis

My DIY reproduction cost significantly less than previous reproduction attempts:
- ChatGPT subscription: $20/month
- Claude subscription: $20/month
- Total: $40

ChatGPT o1-pro is not included in the 20$ tier, and the 200$ subscription was beyond my budget for this quick experiment.

Several other groups worked with Kalamang/MTOB. Hus and Anastasopoulos (October 2024) worked with 16 low-resource languages, testing it with GPT-4-turbo and noted that evaluating translation for several low-resource languages cost them $15,000.

The DIY reproduction is notably cheaper than the $15,000, though that study covered multiple languages and used the LLM via API; **DIY reproduction is, of course, dirtier and less rigorous**. 

The costs could be further reduced by completing all testing within a single month's subscription period. I spent approximately:
- 10 hours running tests across all models
- 10 hours analyzing results and writing this blog post

## Conclusion

With just $40 in subscription costs and about 20 hours of work, I was able to validate several key findings from previous studies and noticed some interesting patterns:
1. More context doesn't always guarantee better translations. Claude 3.5 Sonnet consistently outperformed models with larger context window, suggesting that model architecture and training may matter more than raw context size.

2. Models demonstrate surprising consistency in their errors. When they misinterpret a word or phrase, this misinterpretation often persists across different versions, suggesting systematic issues in how they process linguistic information.

3. Even advanced models can struggle with long contexts. This was particularly evident with ChatGPT 4o, which despite its capabilities in other areas, often produced completely unrelated or nonsensical translations.

MTOB remains a valid and interesting benchmark for evaluating LLM translation capabilities. The results show that current state-of-the-art LLMs can provide reasonably good translations from low-resource languages given appropriate reference materials. With a comprehensive grammar book and dictionary, even casual users with no prior knowledge of a language can expect decent translation attempts, though the quality will vary depending on sentence complexity and specific linguistic features.
